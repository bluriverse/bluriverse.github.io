Active Directory audit & hardening

From technical analysis to a clear, prioritised action plan.

This write-up focuses on method: how I go from a technical report to recommendations a team can schedule and leadership can understand. It builds on the work done in the Bluriverse lab.

A complementary angle on the Bluriverse lab — not a separate engagement.

011. Identify

  • Identifying misconfigurations: default settings, legacy protocols, stale accounts, password policies.
  • Privilege analysis: sensitive group membership, delegations, service accounts.
  • Tiering review: where do privileged accounts log on?
  • GPO review: coverage, conflicts, missing security settings.
  • PingCastle audit as an objective, repeatable baseline.

022. Prioritise

Not every finding carries the same weight. I rank them on two axes: security impact (especially on Tier 0) and remediation effort, including the risk of breaking things for users.

Immediate
High impact, low effort: Tier 0 exposure, unneeded legacy protocols.
Planned
High impact, high effort: delegation redesign, tier separation.
Opportunistic
Moderate impact: clean-up, standardisation, documentation.

033. Recommend and remediate

  • Recommendations written as concrete actions, with their rationale and expected impact.
  • Fixes applied in batches, tested, then documented.
  • Preference for measures that can be automated and verified over time.

044. Measure

A new audit after each batch of fixes measures the actual change in posture. In the Bluriverse lab, the PingCastle risk score went from 70 to 20 after remediation.

Result observed in a personal lab, not during a production audit.

05What the approach delivers

  • A readable summary for decision-makers: key risks, priorities, estimated effort.
  • A detailed action plan for technical teams.
  • A tracking indicator to measure progress.
